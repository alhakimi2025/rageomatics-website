Digital Twins: How Surveying and Geomatics Are Shaping the Future of Infrastructure

Published: 2026-03-20 • Author: Eng. Ramzi Alhakimi

A Digital Twin is a high-fidelity virtual replica of physical assets, processes, or systems. It combines real-time data with sensors and AI to simulate, monitor, and continuously improve real-world environments. In 2026, digital twins are no longer a “nice-to-have” concept—they are becoming a critical tool for modernizing infrastructure and improving project outcomes.

Digital Twins in Saudi Arabia’s Mega-Projects

Digital twins are gaining strong momentum in Saudi Arabia as a foundational pillar for smart-city ambitions—especially in transformative initiatives such as NEOM and the Red Sea projects. These mega-projects rely on continuous data streams and advanced simulation workflows to ensure resilience, sustainability, and a seamless user experience.

In addition, this technology helps improve the efficiency of water and energy infrastructure. It can be used to monitor stress and deterioration in bridges and public transportation networks, reducing maintenance cost and increasing operational reliability.

Integrating GIS and BIM to Enable Better Decisions

From a purely engineering standpoint, building a reliable digital twin depends on deep integration between Building Information Modeling (BIM) workflows (e.g., Civil 3D) and Geographic Information Systems (GIS) platforms (e.g., ArcGIS). Leading engineering firms increasingly use ArcGIS environments to host assets and spatial data, while leveraging AI-assisted design approaches to generate terrain surfaces and underground utility layouts more efficiently.
